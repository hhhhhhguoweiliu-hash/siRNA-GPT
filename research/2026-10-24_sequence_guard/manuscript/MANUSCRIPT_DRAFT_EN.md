# siRNA-GPT: a tool-assisted workflow for traceable VEGFA siRNA review

Working manuscript for scientific review. Authors, affiliations, journal format and final data-sharing terms remain to be supplied by the authors. This draft reports retained computational and software evidence, including a known error. It is not a submission-ready claim of experimentally effective siRNA design.

## Abstract

Selecting and checking a small interfering RNA (siRNA) involves exact sequences, strand conventions, target references and tool-specific evidence. Language-model assistance can coordinate these steps but can also introduce transcription errors when describing valid tool outputs. We developed siRNA-GPT, a restricted VEGFA/ARPE-19 assistance prototype with automatic planning, guided execution, scoped literature retrieval and exportable tool records. A retained siDirect output of 104 designs supplied an unchanged 65-design reporting pool; one user-purchased duplex was assessed separately. Existing official-tool calculations covered 66 model inputs and 132 strand-level seed scans. Completing a fixed 264-query near-full plan required 240 additional queries and 27 min 20 s of local execution. Twenty-three designs had a non-target zero- or one-mismatch match in at least one strand/window; absence of such matches in the remaining designs was not interpreted as safety. A genuine purchased-sequence Agent case completed six tools through eight model requests, consuming 39,048 tokens, but its final narrative incorrectly expanded a verified 19-nt passenger core to 24 nt. The original answer was retained. A subsequent deterministic guard displayed verified sequence fields directly and rejected the saved error and a 19-nt single-base-error control while accepting the corresponding correct control in offline replay. These findings support a reviewable computational workflow and illustrate why tool completion and correct scientific interpretation must be evaluated separately. No new siRNA efficacy experiment or independent quality score was obtained.

## Introduction

Sequence-design tools and sequence matching address different parts of siRNA review. siDirect 2.0 reports functional and sequence-specificity annotations [1]; OligoFormer predicts siRNA efficacy-related scores [2]; and SeedMatchR provides seed-region matching functions that can support interpretation of potential off-target relationships [3]. Their outputs have different units and reference assumptions. A model score is not a measured knockdown percentage, a seed site is not a distinct gene, and a computational match is not an observed off-target effect.

Conversational coordination may help users retain those distinctions while moving between inputs, tools, reference sequences and source documents. CRISPR-GPT demonstrated an agent-based gene-editing framework with automatic, guided and question-answering interactions [4]. We adopted that interaction idea for a smaller siRNA assistance task. We did not reproduce its full model-training programme, benchmark or experimental scope, and did not fine-tune a model. Our aim was a restricted and inspectable workflow that helps a user review existing candidates and genuine calculation evidence, rather than autonomously select a clinically or experimentally validated sequence.

The demonstration scope was human VEGFA and existing ARPE-19 baseline expression data. We retained the earlier candidate pool and development records, completed a predefined fixed-reference query plan, and evaluated one authentic user-purchased sequence through the existing hosted Agent. We also added a small source library focused on tool use, score interpretation, strand direction, matching limitations and expression context. Three VEGFA-related experimental papers were retained as contextual evidence [5-7], not as substitutes for experiments in this project.

An error in the genuine case motivated a targeted output-integrity check. The scientific tools received the correct duplex, but the generated explanation transcribed one core incorrectly. This study therefore reports the computational outputs, the incorrect generated text and the later guard as distinct evidence. It asks whether a small workflow can retain and present evidence consistently, not whether the Agent discovers a best siRNA or outperforms other methods.

## Methods

### System and evidence boundaries

siRNA-GPT is a single-owner Python web application that combines a configured language-model API with six scientific tools: candidate inspection, target-reference verification, efficacy-score retrieval or execution, seed-match retrieval or execution, baseline-expression annotation, and report compilation. Automatic mode uses model-based planning and iterative tool calls after the existing user-facing plan-confirmation step. Guided mode uses a predefined dependency path. The fixed-workflow comparator applies a predefined tool path without model-based planning or interpretation. These differences in prior path selection and iterative model use are retained in reporting and do not themselves demonstrate scientific superiority. No model fine-tuning or state-dependent risk reranking was undertaken.

Hosted scoring and seed operations import explicitly versioned local outputs; they are not newly executed heavy computations on the free web server. Source candidates, results and evidence versions are checked before reuse. The purchased-sequence dataset is separate from the historical nine-candidate dataset. Raw answers, failures and earlier development batches are retained unchanged; appended explanations do not replace historical answers.

### Candidate source and fixed target references

The revised candidate pool was obtained from the same retained siDirect 2.0 request and its 104 original output rows. Original Ui-Tei and duplex seed melting-temperature settings were retained, including the original 21.5°C printed boundary annotation. A candidate entered the 65-design reporting pool when both strands had a reported minimum non-target mismatch count of at least two in the original siDirect g2–g20 fields. The historical siDirect database release is unavailable. Consequently, those fields are historical design annotations, not substitutes for checks against the fixed local reference.

Unlike the older nine-candidate working pool, the 65-design reporting pool did not discard candidates solely for core GC content outside 30–65%, any homopolymer of four bases, or incomplete coverage of all 20 retained VEGFA transcript references. These properties and official OligoFormer sequence flags were reported explicitly. The official OligoFormer implementation does include a GC-content flag at 30–65%; the absence of a pool-level GC exclusion must not be described as absence of any official GC rule. Pool inclusion is distinct from suitability for an experiment.

NM_003376.6 was the computational demonstration transcript. Exact matches of the 19-nt paired core were reported separately across the 20 fixed VEGFA references. Coordinates refer to a 1-based closed 19-nt core interval; original siDirect 23-nt design-window coordinates are separate fields. Transcript-reference coverage is not a measured proportion of VEGFA suppression or isoform abundance.

### Local OligoFormer and seed calculations

Previously completed local inference covered all 65 designs and the purchased duplex as a separate entry. The pinned OligoFormer implementation and retained weights were used through the documented CPU adaptation, with an explicit 19-nt guide core and 57-nt target context. Raw class-1 outputs, the official 1.341 score scaling and func_filter annotations were retained. These scores have not been calibrated to knockdown measurements in this project; scores above one are not percentages. No inference was repeated for the near-full follow-up.

SeedMatchR 1.1.1, with Biostrings 2.70.1 under R 4.3.1, was previously executed for both strands of all 66 entries using mer7m8, zero mismatches and no indels. The fixed reference contained one representative annotated 3′ UTR for each of 19,415 genes. All 132 strand units retained counts including zero rows (2,562,780 rows in total), together with site-level results. Site counts, deduplicated matched-gene counts, expression-mapped genes and state-detected genes are distinct quantities. The VEGFA target gene (Entrez 7422) is excluded from non-target summaries. This is sequence-based seed matching, not experimental off-target validation.

### Completion of the fixed near-full query plan

The original plan contained 264 unique queries: 66 entries × two strands × two 19-nt windows. Each strand was checked using its paired-core positions 1–19 and original 21-nt sequence positions 2–20. The second window follows the siDirect near-full definition; the first is a project consistency check. The windows overlap by 18 bases and are not independent algorithms or experiments.

Queries were reverse-complemented to target patterns and searched against the same fixed 186,185-transcript RNA reference using Biostrings vmatchPattern, max.mismatch=2, with.indels=FALSE and fixed=TRUE. Actual mismatch counts and 1-based transcript positions were retained. One raw match extended one base beyond the reference end; it was preserved, labelled as an 18-base boundary overlap and checked with the installed official neditStartingAt function. A separate full-window-only summary excludes this boundary overlap without altering raw output; all 0/1-mismatch findings are unaffected. Transcript-to-gene mapping preserved all mapped gene identifiers and explicitly labelled unknown mappings. Cumulative site and non-target-gene summaries were generated for at most zero, one and two mismatches. Unknown mapping was not treated as absence of expression.

The first batch completed 24 queries covering the five coding-region designs and purchased entry; its 15-minute budget was an operational limit, not a biological exclusion. The follow-up derived the exact 240-query difference from the 264-item plan and those 24 completed keys. The older 256-item intermediate list was not rerun because 16 of its entries had subsequently been completed. New raw results, commands, reference fingerprints, loaded package versions, start/end times, exit status and zero-hit states were retained in a separate directory. The final merge independently rechecked stored sites, mismatch counts, reference order, gene mappings and cumulative summaries. The final completion audit established coverage of all 264 unique queries; intermediate checkpoints were not used as final results.

### Purchased duplex and baseline expression

The user-provided original synthesis document records row 1 as `GAUAGAGCAAGACAAGAAATT-N3` with an azide note and row 2 as `UUUCUUGUCUUGCUCUAUCTT`. Both original strings and modification notes were retained. Assuming both rows are written 5′ to 3′, their first 19 bases are complementary and row 2 is the putative guide. These roles are derived, not supplier-confirmed. The literal TT characters were not silently interpreted as dTdT or converted into an asserted chemical specification. The exact N3 attachment and complete assembled construct were not simulated.

The putative guide was bound to NM_003376.6 positions 1439–1457, its 57-nt context, the fixed-reference fingerprint and the original computation records before import into the six-tool workflow. Changed sequences, original-chain fields, coordinates or reference versions stop reuse. The server retains imported evidence with the exported report, including both-strand seed and near-full data. The baseline expression join uses guide seed hits; passenger results remain separately labelled supplementary evidence.

Expression data derive from eight baseline ARPE-19 culture dishes from one culture batch, four normoxic and four exposed to 1% O₂ for 24 h. They are not four independent culture rounds and were not automatically paired by sample number. A gene was labelled detected within a condition when TPM was at least one in at least two of its four dishes. TPM is descriptive baseline context, not siRNA-perturbation evidence and not a score or risk weight.

### Literature retrieval and development records

The scoped library contains 12 source materials representing three tool papers, three experimental papers, official documentation/source excerpts and a project record. Multiple formats of the same paper are not counted as independent papers. Chinese queries retrieve English source passages using the existing lightweight index; generated citations must correspond to retrieved text with a page or text-fragment locator. Project settings, local calculation records and literature findings remain separate source types. Five abstract XPath metadata errors were corrected and all 90 Chen/Osipova block locators were verified against unchanged XML text. The Kleinman material is the acquired 15-page author manuscript; its unavailable supplementary sequence table is not reconstructed.

Earlier 6-task × 3-arm × 3-repeat development records (54 planned units) and failed answers remain historical evidence for their respective code versions. Repeats are technical repetitions rather than independent questions. Independent human quality scores remain unfilled; software/tool completion cannot be used as scientific accuracy. The new literature feature and purchased case are not retroactively added to that old batch. The previously completed day-7 missing-measurement question is a separate real library regression, not an experimental knockdown measurement.


### Deterministic sequence check after model interpretation

After the reported real case, a sequence-consistency guard was added to future final-answer handling. A canonical sequence table is built directly from successful candidate-inspection and target-verification fields, together with unchanged original strand strings. The table is not regenerated by the language model. The original TT and N3 annotation and the conditional strand-assignment basis are retained verbatim.

The guard extracts contiguous nucleotide-like strings of at least ten bases from the generated explanation. It checks exact strings against the verified fields for the current candidate inputs. Explicit guide/passenger, core/full-strand and original-row labels restrict comparison to the corresponding fields; an explicitly named candidate restricts comparison to that candidate. A mismatch produces a separate failed sequence-check state and a prominent evidence-derived display. The unmodified model text, its digest and the discrepancy are preserved separately. The scientific-tool completion state is not changed into a claim of a correct answer. Chat, HTML and JSON use the same validation object.

Validation used the saved 24-nt erroneous answer, an edited control with a single-base error but length 19 nt, and a control replacing only the erroneous string with the correct verified core. The latter two are deterministic test fixtures, not new language-model responses. Additional software checks cover swapped roles, an original 21-nt strand mislabelled as a 19-nt core, absence of verified inputs and absence of long-sequence claims. The replay reuses saved tool evidence without calling a model or rerunning a scientific tool. It does not retroactively change the real case or its answer.

This guard is deliberately limited. It does not establish scientific correctness of numerical or mechanistic claims, confirm chemical modifications, inspect all short seed strings, or resolve every natural-language statement about direction. Ambiguous candidate association in a multi-candidate narrative is recorded; matching an allowed pool sequence alone is not a general semantic proof. The original literature-question-answering scope is separate from candidate final-answer validation.

## Results

### Complete pool and offline evidence

The unchanged reporting rule retained 65 of the original 104 designs: five coding-region designs, nine 5′-UTR/boundary designs and 51 3′-UTR designs. The historical nine are a subset. All 66 model predictions and all 132 strand-level seed computations were completed previously; the follow-up did not recalculate them. Recomputed historical-control scores and flags matched the older nine, and their 14,524 positive guide-seed gene-count rows matched the older import.

The follow-up completed the remaining 240 unique queries in 1,640.096 s (27 min 20.096 s), with exit code zero. Together with the original 24, all 264 queries were covered: 246 had raw hits and 18 had zero hits, with no failures or unrun units. All 7,480 stored raw site rows and cumulative summaries were checked; 7,479 intervals lie fully within the transcript reference, while one boundary overlap is reported separately. At least one mapped non-target hit with zero or one mismatch occurred in one or more queries for 23 of 65 designs, including 21 newly examined designs; no unknown-mapping hits were found. The other 42 designs are not thereby established as safe. Full tables, query states, raw results and derived full-window-only summaries are retained in `RESULTS_TABLES.md` and accompanying data. No new pool rule or best-of-65 ranking was inferred.

### Purchased-sequence evidence and real Agent case

The purchased core matched 18 of the 20 fixed VEGFA references. Its raw class-1 score was 0.8366401195526123 and scaled score was 1.121934400320053, with official flag zero. Guide seed results contained 3,540 sites in 2,840 genes including VEGFA, or 3,539 sites in 2,839 non-target genes. Passenger results contained 1,402 sites in 1,286 non-target genes. Across all four near-full queries, there were no mapped non-target or unknown-mapping hits at zero or one mismatch; the at-most-two-mismatch non-target gene counts were 1, 3, 3 and 3 for guide core, guide g2–g20, passenger core and passenger g2–g20, respectively. These are properties of the retained references, not evidence of experimental specificity or conjugate performance.

One real automatic-mode case (20261024_121627_1e50c9, deployed code 03092ef) completed all six tools after the existing plan-confirmation step. One planning request and seven execution requests consumed 39,048 tokens (37,760 input and 1,288 output); all eight responses were completed without an incomplete reason. The 57-file original tool export and planning exchange were retained, and 29 deployed asset hashes matched the corresponding Git blobs. However, the generated final answer incorrectly transcribed the 19-nt passenger core as a 24-nt string. The verified input and structured computation records retained the correct 19-nt sequence. The incorrect answer was preserved and a separate evidence-based correction was added without rerunning the case. Workflow completion therefore must not be interpreted as an error-free answer or scientific accuracy. These records and the correction are in the original near-full delivery’s `live/` directory, identified in the accompanying evidence map.

### Candidate discussion and missing validation

1157 and 1555 remain provisional alternatives from the earlier coding-region subgroup, with 20/20 and 14/20 reference coverage, respectively. They are not designated the best candidates among all 65 designs. In particular, the six references not matched by 1555 cannot be assumed irrelevant or unexpressed. The purchased entry provides an authentic user-sequence case with its own evidence version, rather than borrowing a legacy candidate score. Final experimental target scope, supplier-confirmed strand/end chemistry and a laboratory plan remain decisions for the user and scientific review.

No new knockdown measurement, delivery assessment, N3-conjugate assay, independent human score, held-out scientific-accuracy estimate or superiority result was obtained. This work supports a traceable computational assistance prototype within a fixed VEGFA/ARPE-19 scope.


### Historical development records remain unscored

The retained October 22 development batch contains six tasks, three arms and three technical repetitions per task/arm (54 planned units). Direct inspection of the saved record list gives 18 ordinary-model records labelled ANSWERED_NO_TOOLS; the fixed workflow has six COMPLETE, three PARTIAL_COMPLETE and nine FAILED records; and the Agent has six COMPLETE, three PARTIAL_COMPLETE, seven idle and two FAILED records. These are the stored software states, not correctness grades. An idle state or input-dependent stop cannot automatically be classified as a scientific success or failure. The tasks were development materials already seen during implementation, and technical repetition does not create 54 independent questions. No independent human quality scores have been assigned and no superiority comparison is made. Neither the literature component nor the new guard is added retrospectively to these records.

### Targeted sequence-guard replay

The saved real 24-nt error was rejected. The length-preserving single-base-error control was also rejected, demonstrating that the check did not rely only on string length. The corresponding correct-field control passed. In all three saved replays, chat, HTML and JSON contained the same check state; original model text remained separate from the evidence-derived sequence table. The table retained literal TT/N3 annotations and the conditional role assumption. These three deterministic controls are software regression evidence, not an independent test set or an estimate of error-detection sensitivity. No new model request or OligoFormer, seed or near-full calculation was performed for the replay.

## Discussion

The prototype produced a complete, traceable computational chain within a restricted target scope. Completing all 264 planned queries removed the earlier operational asymmetry between coding-region and other candidate entries without changing the 65-entry pool rule. Full plan coverage is useful for consistent reporting, but it does not establish comprehensive specificity. The four windows are correlated checks, and all results depend on the retained transcript and gene-mapping references. The single reference-boundary overlap illustrates why raw search output and its biological interpretation should remain distinguishable.

The 65 designs form a reporting pool rather than a validated experiment-ready set. The 23 zero-/one-mismatch flags identify entries requiring further review; they are not measurements of off-target silencing. Conversely, the 42 unflagged entries are not certified safe. Coverage of 20 retained transcripts is an annotation of reference sequence membership, not a measured share of total VEGFA expression or suppression. Candidates 1157 and 1555 remain provisional discussion alternatives from the earlier coding-region subgroup, not a validated best-of-65 ranking. The experiment's actual transcript and measurement objectives must be set by the investigator.

The purchased duplex was a useful authentic-input case because it could not be represented by borrowing an old candidate's score. Exact input/reference binding connected it to its own local calculation records. Nevertheless, the score described an unmodified 19-nt core and target context; it did not simulate N3 attachment, the assembled construct, delivery, RISC loading, or experimental potency. Supplier confirmation of strand/end conventions, chemistry and quality information remains outstanding. The experimental literature provides contextual conditions and mechanism cautions [5-7], but neither its knockdown values nor disease-model findings are results of this project.

The observed narrative error is a substantive result of the engineering evaluation. Correct tool inputs and successful tool execution did not prevent the model from producing an incorrect sequence string. The later guard reduces this particular failure mode by giving users an evidence-derived table and exposing mismatches. It does not repair the historical answer or turn it into a correct example. Only offline saved-answer controls were performed after this repair; there is no claim that a new real model case has validated the deployed guard's general performance.

Several limitations restrict the article's conclusions. The system uses a hosted model API whose costs and availability are external dependencies. Heavy hosted computations are verified local imports, not a general cloud backend for arbitrary sequences. The candidate and expression demonstrations concern VEGFA and ARPE-19; the baseline expression dishes came from one culture batch and were not siRNA-treated. The source library is intentionally small, some historical acquisition/command details remain unavailable, and the retained Kleinman author manuscript does not include the missing supplementary sequence table. The historical development tasks are not held-out evaluation. No independent human quality scoring, knockdown measurement, conjugate-performance assay or new animal experiment was performed.

Within those limits, a small assistance article can report the complete computational materials, the real user-sequence case and its observed error, and a targeted integrity repair. Broader model training, state-risk weighting, automatic ordering and larger wet-laboratory programmes were outside the agreed scope. Further scientific choices require investigator review of actual objectives and evidence rather than changes made to obtain a desired number of shortlisted sequences.

## Conclusions

siRNA-GPT coordinates fixed-scope siRNA review while retaining original inputs, tool evidence and model exchanges. The completed candidate computations support descriptive sequence/reference reporting; the authentic Agent case demonstrates both a working six-tool chain and a real transcription error. A subsequent deterministic guard detects that saved error and a length-preserving control while preserving the original response. These results support inspectable assistance, not validated siRNA efficacy, safety or Agent superiority.
