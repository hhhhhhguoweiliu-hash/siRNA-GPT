# Figure legends

Figure 1 | Submitted expert assessments of standard siRNA evidence tasks. The four seen development scenarios were W1, W2, W2b and W3. Panels a and b show submitted forms A and B separately. Bars show arithmetic means of the four task scores for each configuration and dimension; open circles show all task scores on the original 0–2 ordinal scale. Horizontal offsets separate overlapping circles and do not alter scores. The underlying model was the same for the ordinary LLM and Agent; only the two tool configurations accessed the specified scientific tools and bound evidence. Differences therefore concern the configured systems, not an isolated causal effect of reasoning. The two forms have not been pooled or assumed to represent independent raters. Rating–source discrepancies remain unresolved.

Figure 2 | Task completion labels in the submitted forms. Rows show forms A and B; columns show standard tasks (four scenarios), requirement change (one two-round episode), and reliability checks (four scenarios). Each bar is 100 times the number labelled completed divided by the number of scenarios in that stratum, with the numerator and denominator shown above. Partial completion is not counted as completed. Form B labels describing completion of the available portion in R4 were mapped to completed without altering their original text. These are descriptive, unadjudicated submitted labels, not validated biological accuracy or efficacy rates. No significance tests or trial-based error bars were calculated.

Supplementary Figure S1 | Four-dimensional scores for the four seen reliability scenarios R1–R4. Forms are shown separately; bars are means and circles show individual task scores. These cases examine specific reliability behaviors and are not pooled with ordinary research-task completion. They do not establish general safety performance.

Supplementary Figure S2 | Scores for the single seen requirement-change episode U1. Both rounds belong to one episode. Each circle and bar represents that one episode's submitted score, not independent rounds or repeated trials. No generalization across unseen requirement changes is claimed.

Design reference: Qu et al., CRISPR-GPT for agentic automation of gene-editing experiments, Nature Biomedical Engineering 10, 245–258 (2026), Figure 4f, DOI 10.1038/s41551-025-01463-z, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-025-01463-z . The multi-dimension grouped comparison is adapted for the present three configurations and 0–2 rubric. The published paper's three independent trials, 1–5 ratings, numerical improvements and error bars were not copied into this dataset.
